## Project Summery:

INX Future Inc, (referred to as INX ), is one of the leading data analytics and automation solutions providers with over 15 years of global business presence. INX is consistently rated as top 20 best employers past 5 years. INX human resource policies are considered employee friendly and widely perceived as best practices in the industry.

In recent years, employee performance indexes are not healthy and this is becoming a growing concern among the top management. There have been increased escalations in service delivery and client satisfaction levels came down by 8 percentage points.

CEO, Mr Brain, knows the issues but is concerned to take any actions in penalizing non-performing employees as this would affect the employee morale of all the employees in general and may further reduce the performance. Also, the market perception best employer thereby attracts the best talents to join the company.

Mr Brain decided to initiate a data science project, which analyses the current employee data and finds the core underlying causes of these performance issues. Mr Brain, being a data scientist himself, expects the findings of this project will help him to take the right course of action. He also expects clear indicators of non-performing employees, so that any penalization of the non-performing employee, if required, may not significantly affect other employee's morals.

## Data Analysis Report:

### 📊 Key Business Insights

Overall Workforce Overview

•	The organization consists of 1,200 employees, with 178 employees leaving, resulting in an overall attrition rate of 15%. 
•	The average employee is 37 years old, with 7 years of experience, indicating a relatively mature workforce. 
•	Employees received an average 15% salary hike, yet attrition remains significant, suggesting that compensation alone is not the primary driver of employee turnover. 
________________________________________
### Employee Attrition Insights

**1. Sales Department Experiences the Highest Attrition**

•	Nearly 40% of all employee attrition comes from the Sales department. 
•	Research & Development is the second highest contributor, while HR, Finance, and Data Science show comparatively lower attrition. 
Business Recommendation
•	Conduct department-specific engagement surveys. 
•	Review workload distribution, incentive structures, and career progression within Sales. 
________________________________________
**2. Single Employees Leave More Frequently**
   
•	Single employees account for the majority of resignations, significantly higher than married or divorced employees. 
Possible Interpretation
•	Younger employees may be more willing to switch jobs for career growth and better opportunities. 
Recommendation
•	Improve career development programs. 
•	Introduce mentorship and internal promotion pathways. 
________________________________________
**3. Business Travel Strongly Influences Attrition**

Employees who travel frequently experience noticeably higher attrition than employees who rarely travel.

**This suggests:**

•	Work-life imbalance 
•	Increased stress 
•	Higher burnout risk 
Recommendation
•	Review travel policies. 
•	Introduce hybrid collaboration where possible. 
•	Offer travel compensation and wellness initiatives. 
________________________________________
**4. Overtime is a Major Risk Factor**

Employees working overtime leave at a much higher rate than employees who do not work overtime.
This clearly indicates that excessive workload contributes significantly to employee turnover.

Recommendation
•	Monitor overtime hours. 
•	Redistribute workload. 
•	Hire additional resources where necessary. 
________________________________________
**5. Poor Work-Life Balance Correlates with Higher Attrition**

Employees reporting lower work-life balance ratings show noticeably higher attrition.
This confirms that employee well-being has a measurable impact on retention.
________________________________________
**6. Lower Job Satisfaction Leads to Higher Employee Turnover**

Employees with low job satisfaction scores exhibit substantially higher attrition than employees with higher satisfaction ratings.

Recommendation
•	Conduct quarterly employee satisfaction surveys. 
•	Improve recognition programs. 
•	Strengthen manager-employee communication. 
________________________________________
### Demographic Insights

**7. Young Employees Leave More Frequently**

The 26–35 age group represents the largest share of employee attrition.

Possible reasons include:
•	Faster career progression 
•	Better salary opportunities 
•	Skill development elsewhere 
________________________________________
**8. Male Employees Show Higher Attrition**

Male employees account for a larger proportion of employee exits than female employees.
While gender alone should not drive HR decisions, further analysis could determine whether this is related to department distribution, travel frequency, or overtime.
________________________________________
**9. Employees with 2–5 Years of Tenure are Most Likely to Leave**

Attrition peaks among employees with approximately 2–5 years of company experience.

This is a common stage where employees:
•	Seek promotions 
•	Expect salary growth 
•	Explore external opportunities 

Retention initiatives should therefore focus on employees before they reach this tenure milestone.

The highest employee turnover occurs in:
•	Sales Executive

These positions should receive priority attention for retention strategies.

### Performance Insights

**12. Performance Rating Alone Does Not Explain Attrition**

Employees across multiple performance ratings leave the organization, indicating that attrition is influenced by several interacting factors rather than performance alone.

Likely drivers include:
•	Overtime 
•	Business travel 
•	Job satisfaction 
•	Career growth 
•	Work-life balance 
________________________________________
### Executive Summary

The analysis indicates that employee attrition at INX Future Inc. is driven more by workplace experience than by compensation. Although employees receive an average 15% salary hike, turnover remains at 15%, suggesting that non-financial factors have a stronger influence on retention.

The most significant contributors to attrition include:
•	High overtime workload 
•	Frequent business travel 
•	Poor work-life balance 
•	Low job satisfaction 
•	Sales department concentration 
•	Employees in the early stages of their careers (26–35 years old with 2–5 years of tenure) 

Addressing these areas through targeted HR policies, employee engagement initiatives, career development opportunities, and workload optimization can significantly improve employee retention, organizational performance, and client satisfaction.
